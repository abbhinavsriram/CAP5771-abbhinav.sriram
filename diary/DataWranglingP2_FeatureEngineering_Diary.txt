We engineered a few features to help us with removing excess articles and further categorization of the articles. First, we combined the title with a preview of the body text into a single `combined_text` field so that each article had one consistent representation for clustering and topic modeling.

We first used clustering to identify groups of articles that were irrelevant to our problem formulation. We chose this because it was a computationally efficient way (compared to using an LLM to score each article) to significantly reduce the amount of noise in tehd data. We ended up retaining the cluster assignments as a feature for later exploration. 

For developer articles, we noticed that some technical discussions remained in our data. Therefore, we ran a zero-shot classification (`TECHNICAL` vs. `NON_TECHNICAL`) to further filter out unrelated articles.

After removing irrelevant articles, we ran a zero-shot classification algorithm to categorize the articles into one of 7 labels (environment, jobs, law, safety, etc.). This part, specifically, is crucial for our analysis.

In the end, we reduced the number of developer articles from 25,672 articles -> 5,398 articles, and the number of news articles from 15023 -> 10457 for sentiment analysis.
