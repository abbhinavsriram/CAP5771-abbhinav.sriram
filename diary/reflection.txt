1. Dev posts
The acquisition of the data was very difficult since we were trying to pull more conceptual discussion articles from a site which had a large concentation of technical-focused discussions without many options to filter on the server side through the API. We initially tried to just pull data from articles tagged with the "ai" keyword. However, we began to realize that this wasn't fully capturing our desired timeline (our data would only stretch back to 2023 since articles earlier than this year were improperly tagged). Therefore, we began to focus our search on find filters that would exclude articles that might not be a good fit, rather than trying to find a set of filters that would magically lead us to the right set of articles. It took multiple attempts for us to create a set of two keyword lists. One list consisted of manually handpicked tags that were less likely to contain discussions about AI (for instance, articles containing tags like php, kubernetes were excluded). Furthermore, we also created a keyword list consiting of words pertaining to our discussion focuses (context window issues, environment, etc.), requiring that any article that had at least 3 distinct words from our keyword list be added to our database, with anything less being removed from consideration. As a result of these filters, we were able to significantly reduce the number of articles we stored in our database from hundreds of thousands to ~3000 relevant articles.

However, after further data exploration, we realized that keyword matching might not be the only filter that we need. For instance, articles talking about 'environment' weren't discussing the environemntal impact of AI, but instead discussing developer CLIs. For next steps, we anticipate using vector embeddings and semantic searches to identify these anomalous articles by attempting to capture more deeper meanings rather than spelling similarity.
